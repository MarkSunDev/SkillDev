Common pattern: eligibility and prohibited conduct; user content and license;
reporting/moderation; subscriptions and virtual items; account termination;
disclaimers and liability; indemnity; informal dispute resolution followed by
arbitration and a class-action waiver; governing law and venue; changes;
miscellaneous. The strongest dating-app policies add explicit safety,
background-check/ID-verification, state-specific, and app-store sections.

## Privacy Policy structure

**Tinder** — 12 sections: who we are; scope; data we collect; why/how we use it;
how we share; Match Group companies; cross-border transfers; your rights;
retention; children's privacy (18+); changes; how to contact us (regional
contacts and postal addresses).

**Bumble** — collection categories (registration, social-media linking, photo/ID
verification including biometrics, purchases, geolocation, device, photos,
links, messages, success stories/surveys, support contacts, cookies); use and
algorithms; lawful basis; BeePitched; sharing; CCPA/state rights; retention and
"What happens when you delete your account?"; age restriction; international
transfers; contact (DPO@bumble.com, feedback@team.bumble.com,
GDPRrequest@privacyquery.ie).

**Hily** — controller/processors; children's privacy; collection; retention,
location and cross-border transfer; tracking technologies; privacy rights;
California and other state notices; access/erasure requests; third-party
websites; security; contact (legal@hily.com, support@hily.com, eu@hily.com,
gdpr@hily.com). States that profile data is deleted or anonymized within 7 days
of profile deletion, backups may persist up to 30 days, and inactive data is
removed after two years.

Common pattern: controller identity and contact; categories of data (including
sensitive dating-app data and user content); sources; purposes; legal bases
where relevant; sharing and processors; sale/share/targeted-advertising
choices; cookies and device identifiers; retention categories; user rights and
verification; children; international transfers; security; changes; contact.

## Account deletion pages

**Tinder**

- In-app: profile icon → Settings → Delete Account.
- Web: "Manage My Account" tool; requires sign-in.
- 90-day safety retention window; profile may be restorable within 90 days.
- Uninstalling the app does not delete the account.
- Deleting the account does not cancel an active subscription.
- Data is deleted in accordance with the Privacy Policy after the window.

**Bumble**

- In-app: profile icon → Settings → Delete account → choose a reason →
  confirm.
- If the in-app flow fails, contact support; the team verifies ownership.
- 28-day restore window; after that, deletion of personal information begins.
- Uninstalling the app or inactivity does not delete the profile.
- Subscriptions may not cancel automatically; cancel them first.
- Privacy Policy explains what happens on deletion and retention.

**Hily**

- In-app: My Profile → Settings → Account → Manage account → Delete account →
  choose a reason → Proceed.
- "Hide account" is a pause, not deletion.
- Hiding or deleting does not cancel a subscription; cancel in Google Play or
  the App Store, or contact support@hily.com.
- Privacy Policy states profile data is deleted/anonymized within 7 days,
  backups up to 30 days.

Common pattern: in-app path first; a web/support path for users who cannot sign
in; explicit distinction between hide/pause, logout, uninstall, and deletion;
restore/cancellation window; subscription cancellation as a separate step;
what is deleted and what is retained; contact channel.

## Child safety and moderation

- **Tinder** terms prohibit child abuse and exploitation, and state that
  moderation prioritizes the most serious violations such as child sexual
  exploitation material. Reports go through in-app reporting; terms also
  address appeals and account bans.
- **Bumble** terms include underage-use monitoring and termination, a Colorado
  safety policy, optional background checks and photo/ID verification (only if
  the app actually offers them), and reporting/blocking.
- **Hily** terms contain an explicit zero-tolerance statement for child sexual
  abuse/exploitation and say potential child-endangerment cases are reported to
  the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC); users are
  directed to the in-app reporting system and the Safety / Reporting page.

Pattern to adopt: explicit 18+ rule; zero tolerance for CSAM and grooming;
in-app reporting plus a dedicated child-safety contact; block/report tools;
removal and account termination; cooperation with law enforcement and NCMEC;
appeals; policy updates. Do not claim background checks, ID verification, or
age-verification technology unless the build actually has it.

## Contact and identity patterns

- Mature policies use role-based addresses and forms, not one consumer inbox:
  legal@, support@, privacy/GDPR@, DPO@, child-safety/safety@, plus help-center
  forms and postal addresses.
- They identify the contracting entity or group (Tinder/Match Group; Bumble
  Holding Limited, Bumble Trading LLC, Bumble Inc.; Hily's controller).
- Our skill should require the developer legal name and a dedicated CSAM
  mailbox, while allowing a single general contact plus optional role addresses.

## Dispute resolution and governing law

- **Tinder:** Delaware law; NAM arbitration; Wilmington, Delaware; 60-day
  informal resolution; class/jury waiver; mass-arbitration protocols.
- **Bumble:** Texas law; Travis County, Texas; arbitration with a 60-day
  informal notice/conference, opt-out, class waiver, and mass-filing process.
- **Hily:** California law; San Francisco County; NAM arbitration; 60-day
  informal resolution; EU residents use Cyprus/Limassol and arbitration does
  not apply.

Do not hard-code Delaware/AAA/Wilmington. Ask for governing law, arbitration
provider, venue, opt-out window, and whether mass-arbitration terms are
required. Defaults are acceptable only when the user confirms them.

## Gaps in the pre-benchmark skill

1. Covered only Terms + Privacy; no Child Safety Policy or Account Deletion page.
2. Imposed one contact email; no dedicated CSAM or privacy/DPO addresses.
3. Forbade the developer legal name; the child safety page requires it.
4. Hard-coded Delaware/AAA/Wilmington; mature apps vary by entity and market.
5. No reporting/blocking/moderation, safety, background-check, or
   identity-verification sections.
6. No subscription-cancellation, app-store, copyright/DMCA, electronic-records,
   entire-agreement, severability, or state-specific sections.
7. No account-deletion page requirements: in-app path, web path, restore
   window, subscription cancellation, uninstall/logout distinction, retention.
8. No Google Form / hosted-form request workflow or processor disclosure.
9. No preflight that asks the user for missing facts before drafting.
10. No dedicated benchmark or page-level QA for the four-page set.

## What to adopt, what not to copy

Adopt the structure: eligibility, content rules, reporting/moderation,
subscriptions, termination/deletion, safety disclaimers, IP/DMCA, third-party
app stores, dispute resolution, governing law, changes, miscellaneous.

Add only if the build supports it: background checks, ID/photo verification,
biometrics, precise location, push notifications, virtual items, ads.

Never copy verbatim: entity names, addresses, emails, retention periods,
provider names, arbitration terms, state-specific commitments, safety claims,
or age-verification claims. Those are facts that must come from the user and
the current build.
